The [first post](/research/carry-or-dispersion/) took apart a funding-carry book on 23
crypto perpetuals: long the eleven coins with the lowest funding, short the eleven with
the highest, hedge the BTC beta. The backtests had Sharpe ratios of 1.32 and 1.24, the
live shadow ledger lost money, and the reason was that the book was never mainly a
carry strategy. Its funding income was a small, steady tailwind; more than 99.9% of
its risk sat in a bet on how individual coins move around the market.

That post ended with one lead: almost all of the carry came from the short leg. So the
natural next step was to short the high-funding perpetuals and hedge each one with
the same coin on spot.

By then I had already tested five variants after seeing a live loss. Testing more
until something looks good is the textbook way to fool yourself, so this round ran
under **pre-registration**: the configurations, the metrics and the rules for what
counts as an improvement were committed to the repository before any test was run.
This post covers two such studies and the live evidence between them.

## Study one: can the book be fixed?

Three choices, fully crossed, give 24 configurations per venue:

- **Signal**: rank on the latest funding print, or on the mean of the last 3 or 7 days.
- **Tranches**: one book rebalanced every 7 days, or seven sub-books on seven
  consecutive days.
- **Construction**: the baseline; inverse-volatility weights with a BTC and ETH hedge;
  correlation pairs; and **cash-and-carry**, short the 11 highest-funding perpetuals
  and long the same coins on spot.

Binance 2021 to 2025 selects, Bybit 2022 to 2025 confirms, and the five live months of
2026 are a holdout used once. The winner is the configuration with the best Sharpe
ratio on its *weaker* venue, and it only counts if it beats the baseline on both.

<Callout title="Found while validating the engine">
  51% of Bybit funding prints are exactly 0.01%, the default rate. In 70 of 148
  rebalances the line between the long and the short leg fell inside such a tie, so
  which coins entered the book depended on the order of the coin list. That is one
  reason the old book was so sensitive to the rebalance day.
</Callout>

Every change except cash-and-carry helps on one venue and hurts on the other. The best
combination inside the old family beats the baseline in-sample by a hair (1.35 and 1.40
against 1.32 and 1.24) and then returned **−11.9%** on the holdout, against +0.4% for
the baseline. Without a rule fixed in advance, that combination would have been easy to
adopt.

Depending only on the day and funding time it rebalances on, the baseline ends the
five months anywhere from −32.2% to +31.0%. A dispersion bet is that sensitive to which
coins it happens to hold. Cash-and-carry's spread across start days is under 2 points.

## Cash-and-carry: the right construction in the wrong regime

| | Sharpe, Binance | Sharpe, Bybit | Return /yr | Max drawdown |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Baseline | 1.32 | 1.24 | 42% / 32% | −32% / −23% |
| Best of the old family | 1.35 | 1.40 | 29% / 26% | −26% / −23% |
| **Cash-and-carry** | **5.96** | **6.39** | 15% / 8% | −2.3% / −0.8% |

Holding the same coin long on spot and short on the perpetual removes the dispersion
bet by construction. Volatility drops from about 25% a year to 1.2%. The deflated
Sharpe ratio over all 48 trials is 0.994. The 8-hour Sharpe ratios overstate, because
funding is persistent; on monthly returns they are 2.21 and 2.89. Still excellent.

The problem shows up by year.

Carry was 40.4% in 2021, 14% in 2024, then 4% in 2025, below the cash yield, and 2.0%
in the 2026 holdout, below costs: the holdout returned −0.7%. Funding is the price
leveraged longs pay for leverage. When they stop demanding it, there is nothing to
harvest. Under the protocol, cash-and-carry became a third live ledger on 7 November.

| | Baseline | Variant C | Cash-and-carry |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Days live | 164 | 11 | 10 |
| Total return | −21.7% | −7.8% | −0.12% |
| Carry, annualised | 5.6% | | 0.6% |

Since the first post the baseline lost another 7.6% in 11 days, now on the clean
schedule and in a steady slide rather than a shock. After 164 days it stands at −21.7%:
carry +2.5%, price −25.1%.

**Is that significant?** With the standard error used throughout this series, which
allows for non-normal returns, the live Sharpe ratio of −1.77 is 1.5 standard errors
below the backtest. Only 0.6% (Binance) and 0% (Bybit) of 164-day backtest windows lost
as much. But the schedule alone spans more than 60 points over five months. Unusual,
not proof.

**Why I froze it anyway.** The decision rests on the economics, not on a z-score:

1. **Net of costs, the carry is about zero.** The ledger charges no costs. Its 5.6% a
   year of carry equals the 5.5% a year the same book paid in costs over the holdout.
2. **What is left is the dispersion bet**, whose historical profit came from one or two
   90-day windows, and which no construction in study one made robust.
3. **Variant C failed at its one job.** Its returns correlate 0.96 with the baseline and
   its price moves are no smaller (0.67% against 0.65% per step).
4. **More data cannot change the decision.** With a schedule spread this wide, it would
   take years of live data to tell a small premium from noise.

The two cross-sectional ledgers stopped on 17 November. The decision was committed
before the second study ran, so its result could not influence it.

<Callout title="A correction">
  The freeze note in the second pre-registration says the live loss is "about two
  standard errors" below the backtest. That used the simple standard error. With the
  non-normality-adjusted one it is 1.5. The note stays as it is, as the protocol
  requires; this is the correction.
</Callout>

## Study two: a rule for when to stand aside

If cash-and-carry only pays in some regimes, the obvious question is whether it can
tell which regime it is in. Cash-and-carry already leaves a slot empty when funding is
not positive, but a coin earning 2% a year still costs trading and ties up capital that
could earn cash.

The second pre-registration adds one parameter: hold a coin only while its 7-day mean
funding, annualised, clears a **hurdle** of 0% (the live control), 5%, 10% or 15%.
Unused capital earns the cash yield, so the metric is the return **over cash**. The
same selection rule applies: best on the weaker venue, and it must beat the control on
both.

| Hurdle | Venue | Sharpe (8h) | Sharpe (monthly) | Over cash /yr | In use |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 0% | Binance | 4.62 | 1.65 | 10.9% | 96% |
| 10% | Binance | 4.87 | 1.67 | 10.9% | 47% |
| **15%** | Binance | **6.74** | 1.42 | 9.0% | 22% |
| 0% | Bybit | 2.79 | 1.31 | 3.4% | 98% |
| 10% | Bybit | 4.62 | 1.69 | 4.1% | 52% |
| **15%** | Bybit | **5.76** | 1.44 | 3.1% | 15% |

The rule picks the 15% hurdle, which beats the control on both venues. On the holdout
no coin cleared 15% in five months, so the filtered book sat in cash throughout: **0.0%
over cash**, against **−5.3% a year** for the unfiltered book.

Three caveats keep this modest:

- **The metric flatters a book that is mostly in cash.** A period in cash has an excess
  return of exactly zero, which lowers the volatility of 8-hour returns. On monthly
  returns the 10% hurdle is slightly better than 15% on both venues. The protocol chose
  15% and that stands; 10% is the more balanced setting.
- **The filter avoids losses, it does not create returns.** The gain is in 2025 and the
  holdout, where it sits in cash instead of paying costs for carry below cash. It also
  gives up most of 2022.
- **Few bad regimes in the sample.** One parameter with a clear economic meaning keeps
  selection risk low, but the data hold only one or two periods of low funding, which
  is exactly what the filter is for.

The 15% hurdle runs as a live shadow ledger from 18 November, next to the unchanged
cash-and-carry ledger. A replay of the past ten days shows it would have been in cash
the whole time; at the time of writing, funding was negative on most of the 23 coins.

## What I take from it

- **A cross-sectional funding book on a small universe is a dispersion bet.** Nothing
  tested here turned it into a carry strategy.
- **Cash-and-carry isolates the funding, and the funding is a regime.** It paid in
  2021 and 2024 and paid less than cash in 2025 and 2026.
- **Cash is the benchmark.** A book with 1 to 2% volatility that earns 4% is not making
  money when cash pays 4%.
- **Pre-registration changed the outcome, not just the write-up.** The best
  combination of the old family would have been adopted, and it returned −11.9% on the
  holdout.
- **Count costs in the live ledger.** Reporting carry before costs made a book with zero
  net carry look like it earned 5.6% a year.

Funding carry is real. The improvement that survived is not a better way to harvest
it, but a rule for when not to.
